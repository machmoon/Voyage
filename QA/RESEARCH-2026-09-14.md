# Research: Voyage against FocusFlight, and an insights feature

September 14, 2026. Two reports from a product-analyst pass and a four-persona user panel,
plus an implementation study for "AI insights". Condensed here; the full reports are in the
session transcript. Everything below is grounded in FocusFlight's App Store pages and site
and in Voyage's own screens and code; nothing is invented on either side.

## Why people pay for FocusFlight (review mentions, directional)

1. App blocking through Screen Time. The Editors' Choice blurb is about it; it is also the
   one reported bug.
2. Cabin white noise and focus music; requests for more sounds.
3. The 3D map window with Apple Maps scenery.
4. Visible accumulation: FlightLog, miles, membership cards, unlocked destinations.
5. A forced minimum length, with pause as the paid escape hatch.

Recurring negatives: too many taps to start a session, "overpriced", features moved behind
the paywall, geography (a 7-hour flight from Australia).

## Where Voyage is already stronger

Physical commitment (tear, 30 s diversion) instead of a setting; real directional block
times, carriers, schedules, connections and a timed lounge; tasks as baggage with claims
and a score; passport stamps, receipts and trip replay; Live Activity and a Siri intent;
procedural cabin audio and recorded PA; a deterministic flight data recorder that refuses
to report below 12 flights; free, no account, on-device.

Honest gaps: no app blocking (the listing promises not to), no pause, ten airports with a
70-minute floor.

## What every persona hit

All four personas (a finals-week sophomore, an ADHD junior on 45-minute sessions, a thesis
student on Pomodoro and Notion, an aviation enthusiast) ran into the same things:

1. **The 70-minute floor.** Nobody could book 45 to 60 minutes. Both reports rank this first.
2. **"What did you work on?" is optional and after the stamp**, so history has no subjects.
3. **Two pre-flight hints conflict**: Focus on the bag screen, Airplane Mode on the pass, and
   Airplane Mode kills the streamed window.
4. **The diverted screen and logbook row read as a detention slip**: "Incomplete · 2m · 0
   miles", and a diverted row shows "+800 mi · 1m" beside a landed flight worth 337.
5. **The lounge is unexplained** on the connection card until the traveler misses it.
6. **No export** of the logbook.
7. Adjustable layover length; flight data in cruise; the padlocked First seats unexplained.

## Recommended work on the existing loop, in order

1. **Short-haul routes**: real regional pairs under 60 minutes from each home airport, same
   catalog shape, surfaced as a "Short haul" row before the die. No minute picker.
2. **Fewer taps to the tear**: "Same seat as last time" preselected, and "Book again" on a
   logbook row that lands on the pass with the last bags prefilled.
3. **Subjects before the stamp**: make "What did you work on?" the first arrival step,
   prefilled from claimed bags.
4. **One pre-flight hint**: Focus mode, not Airplane Mode, since Airplane Mode drops the
   satellite window.
5. **Diverted screen shows what was done** (bags, minutes) and the logbook row stops
   showing misleading miles on diverted flights.
6. **Explain the lounge on the card**: "15 min lounge · 3 min final call".
7. **Holding pattern instead of pause**: once per leg at cruise, up to 5 minutes, visible
   in the window and the Live Activity, recorded in the logbook. Keeps the no-silent-pause
   thesis while answering the one paid feature reviewers name.
8. **Routes flown drawn on the home globe** as faint arcs (a FocusFlight reviewer asked for
   exactly this; Voyage already owns the globe and the arcs).
9. **Claimed-bags ledger** at the top of the logbook status card.

## AI insights: the verdict from both reports

Do not put an LLM in the app. The pitch is "nothing leaves this device", and the flight
data recorder already sets a bar of evidence (12 flights, non-overlapping confidence
intervals) that generated prose would undercut. No review of either app asks for advice.

Ship instead:

1. **Export my logbook** from Settings and the Logbook: one Markdown file, one line per
   flight (date, route, planned and completed minutes, outcome, bags packed and claimed,
   the "what did you work on" note), followed by the recorder's findings.
2. **A prewritten prompt** at the bottom of the export ("Paste this into Claude, ChatGPT or
   Gemini and ask for..."), so the student uses whatever they already have.
3. **Recorder findings on the boarding pass**: one line such as "Your 5 pm flights finish
   bags 2 of 3 times", drawn from the existing detectors.

Revisit on-device generation with Apple's Foundation Models later, seeded with the
recorder's verified findings so the model rewords evidence rather than inventing patterns.
## Implementation study: how to build it

Four paths were checked against real precedents and Apple's docs.

- **Apple Foundation Models (iOS 26)**: fully on-device, 3B model, 4,096-token context,
  iPhone 15 Pro and newer only. Precedent: Dimillian/FoundationChat
  (`FoundationChat/Env/ChatEngine.swift`) checks `SystemLanguageModel.default.availability`
  and streams `@Generable` output. Good later, behind `#available(iOS 26, *)`; most current
  users cannot see it yet.
- **Export and paste**: the pattern shipped by "Health2AI - Export for ChatGPT" and "Health
  Export - AI Data Share", and by nicepkg/ctxport. No chat app has a documented deep link
  that prefills a normal conversation (Claude's `claude://` is Code-only; ChatGPT's `?q=` is
  undocumented; Gemini has none), so ship a `ShareLink` of a String plus a Copy button. Plain
  Markdown, no tables, under about 1,500 tokens so it also fits Apple's window later.
- **User-supplied API key**: about $0.004 per debrief on `claude-haiku-4-5`, but it
  contradicts "nothing leaves this device", needs a 5.1.2(i) consent sheet naming Anthropic,
  and makes students create a paid developer account. Rejected.
- **Deterministic only**: the recorder already does the hard part. Gaps to close: planned
  versus completed minutes, seat, flights per week, longest gap.

**Recommendation: deterministic recorder plus export.** Add a "Debrief" card at the end of
the recorder: one sentence, Copy, Share. The export is the prompt first, then a 90-day
summary, then the recorder's findings restated with their intervals, then the last 20
flights as CSV rows. The user sees the text before it leaves. Model choice: none in-app;
Apple's on-device model as a later fallback; if a direct integration is ever revisited,
`claude-haiku-4-5`.

Export template:

```
You are reading a student's focus-session log from Voyage, an app that
frames study sessions as flights. A flight "lands" when the session runs
to its planned end; it is "diverted" when the app was left or the session
was ended early. "Bags" are tasks the student set out to finish. Use only
the numbers below. Do not invent data, do not diagnose, do not praise.
Give three plain observations and one concrete suggestion for next week.

## Summary (last 90 days, all times local)
- Flights: 34, landed 27 (79%)
- Planned minutes: 2,190; completed: 1,865 (85%)
- Departures by band: 05-09: 3, 09-13: 9, 13-17: 8, 17-21: 11, 21-05: 3
- Bags checked: 41, claimed: 30 (73%)
- Flights per week: 2.6; longest gap: 6 days

## What the recorder found (95% confidence)
- Departure time: flights between 5pm and 9pm reach the gate more often.
  9 of 11 landed, against 18 of 23 at every other hour.

## Recent flights
date, route, planned min, completed min, outcome, bags claimed/total, seat
2026-09-12, SFO-JFK, 90, 90, landed, 2/2, C10
```
