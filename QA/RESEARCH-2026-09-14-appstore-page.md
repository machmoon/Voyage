## 2. Apple guidance and ASO evidence

Apple, product page guidance (https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/): up to 10 screenshots; "the first one to three images will appear in search results when no app preview is available, so make sure these highlight the essence of your app"; each later screenshot should "focus on a main benefit or feature"; include a Dark Mode shot if supported.

Apple, app previews (https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/): up to three previews per locale, 15 to 30 seconds, autoplay on the page and in search; footage captured on device only, no hands or over-the-shoulder shots; choose a poster frame that "conveys the essence of your app"; avoid dated references.

Apple, screenshot specs (https://www.developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/screenshot-specifications/): 6.9-inch portrait 1320 x 2868 (or 1290 x 2796); 6.5-inch 1284 x 2778 or 1242 x 2688; 1 to 10 images, .png/.jpg, no alpha.

Storemaven, 500M+ sessions (https://www.thestoremaven.store/academy/app-store-statistics-revealed/): 60% of visitors never scroll past the first-impression frame; 50% of installers decide from it alone; videos lift installs by over 25% but 98% watch muted and roughly 10% of viewers drop every 5 seconds; portrait galleries drive 13% more exploration than landscape (https://www.storemaven.com/apple-app-store-gallery-landscape-vs-portrait-best-for-cvr/).

SplitMetrics: only 11% of users scroll through all five portrait screenshots (from an analysis of 1,800 A/B tests, as cited at https://appscreenshotstudio.com/blog/panoramic-app-store-screenshots-convert-better-2026); their guide (https://splitmetrics.com/blog/app-store-screenshots-aso-guide/) is specs and practice, no conversion figures.

Appfigures (https://appfigures.com/resources/videos/screenshots-that-convert-live-product-page-teardown): "People don't read long captions"; outcome not function ("Cook anywhere, anytime" over "Your recipe book always with you"); shorter, "chubbier" friendlier fonts; never spend slot 1 on a logo. Appfigures also reported in June 2025 that Apple began extracting screenshot caption text as metadata; ConsultMyApp's controlled test found only 1 of 64 caption phrases ranked on its own (https://appscreenshotstudio.com/blog/how-apple-ocr-indexes-app-store-screenshot-text-2026), so treat captions as a reinforcement of "study timer" and "focus", not a ranking lever.

Panoramas: the "30% lift" figure circulating for panoramic sets has no published study behind it; a panorama only helps when each slice stands alone (same appscreenshotstudio piece). Structured's page is the practical proof: the pair sits at slots 1 and 2.

Practical reading for Voyage: the first three cards carry the decision; 8 cards is plenty; a 15 to 20 second preview of the takeoff roll and window is worth testing later via Product Page Optimization, but a muted, silent video of a study timer is a weaker first impression than a strong still, so ship stills first.

## 3. Recommended 8-slot story for Voyage

Style: dark ground (matches the window at altitude and the category), headline top, one subhead, device frame bleeding off the bottom, one frameless product-shot card (slot 4), laurel-free because there is nothing to claim yet. Keep the current subtitle "A window seat for study time"; it already names the differentiator.

1. Screen: the airplane window mid-cruise, wing visible, haze shader on, a small cabin timer overlay.
   Headline: "A window seat for studying"
   Subhead: "Set a length. Take off. Look up when you land."
   Why: this is the only screen no competitor has (FocusFlight's window is a still photo behind a porthole); slot 1 is the search-result card and the 60% who never scroll.

2. Screen: the globe with the route rail and the departure board sheet half raised, showing real carriers and flight numbers.
   Headline: "Book a real route"
   Subhead: "Actual block times, carriers and departure boards. Eastbound is shorter."
   Why: the "real-world data" hook separates Voyage from random-destination timers and earns the word "real" honestly; second card is still in the search-result trio.

3. Screen: seat map with an over-wing window seat selected, "C10" label.
   Headline: "Pick your seat"
   Subhead: "Window seats over the wing get the wing view."
   Why: a physical, tappable choice with a consequence; it rounds out the first trio with the booking ritual FocusFlight's TikTok reviewers keep filming.

4. Screen: frameless product shot of the printed boarding pass, mid-tear, stub separating.
   Headline: "Tear the boarding pass"
   Subhead: "The session starts when the stub comes off."
   Why: the rhythm-break card (like FocusFlight's floating passes and Flighty's passport); it is also the most GIF-able moment for social.

5. Screen: the moving map in Follow camera, satellite style, plane on the great circle.
   Headline: "Watch the ground go by"
   Subhead: "A moving map you can switch to any time in flight."
   Why: establishes that the flight is a real position, not a countdown skin; pairs with slot 1.

6. Screen: the flight data recorder view with a session timeline and plain-number readouts (start time, phases, where attention broke).
   Headline: "Your own flight recorder"
   Subhead: "Plain records of when you study and where sessions break. No AI, no guesses."
   Why: the deterministic-evidence angle is unique in the set (Opal's "Opal Score" and Forest's charts are the nearest comparisons); saying what it is not is part of the voice.

7. Screen: arrival recap with the baggage-claim belt and the printed receipt.
   Headline: "Land, collect, keep the receipt"
   Subhead: "Every arrival leaves a record you can read later."
   Why: closes the loop of the session; the receipt is a natural share object.

8. Screen: passport page with several stamps, one fresh.
   Headline: "Stamps for every landing"
   Subhead: "Your logbook fills up one flight at a time."
   Why: the collection card at the end mirrors Flighty's Passport and FocusFlight's membership cards, and it is the card most likely to be screenshotted and posted.

Optional panoramic pair: slots 5 and 6 as one wide cabin scene, the window (left card) continuing into the seat-back screen showing the map and recorder (right card), with the horizon line running through both. Test it only as slots 1 and 2 if it is ever moved forward, since the data says a pair nobody reaches costs a strong solo card.

Copy rules kept: no learning-outcome claims, no "productivity", no guilt words ("addictive", "stop losing hours"), no em-dashes.

## 4. Testable share hypotheses

What students actually post, per the TikTok discover page for FocusFlight and Flighty's own channels:
- Monthly tallies: FocusFlight's most-shared format is "POV: you turned your focus time into flights for an entire month, 15 flights, 12h 5min focused" (https://www.tiktok.com/discover/focusflight-app).
- Named real routes: the 781.7K-view clip is a Moscow to Paris study flight; the 12.1M clip is framed around Japan and Korea trips.
- The window and boarding pass: the 27.5K clip describes FocusFlight as "letting you fly to different destinations with a literal boarding pass"; FocusFlight's own channel promotes the window view.
- Flighty's Passport year-in-review is explicitly built as an Instagram Stories card and users post it captioned "2025 Flighty Passport" (https://www.instagram.com/p/DS5w7XQEec2/, https://x.com/Flighty/status/1734623976419135559, https://worldairlinenews.com/2025/12/11/flighty-launches-its-first-year-in-review-global-passport-report-including-the-most-delayed-airlines-in-2025/).

H1. A shareable monthly logbook card (flights, hours, routes flown, stamps, rendered at 9:16) will be posted more than any in-flight screen. Test: ship the card with a share sheet, count share-sheet taps per active user over 30 days against the boarding-pass and window share buttons.

H2. Real route names with the real carrier and flight number (for example "BA 117 LHR to JFK") outperform generic destinations in reach: viewers comment on routes they know. Test: two social clips a week for four weeks, half named-route, half generic, compare views and comments.

H3. The boarding-pass tear is the most GIF-able three seconds and will drive more profile visits than the passport. Test: post the tear as the opening frame of one clip and the passport stamp as the opening frame of another, same hook text, compare 3-second retention and link taps.
